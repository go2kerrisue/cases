The Situation

You can't document what you don't understand

In the late 1990s, Kerri built her first employee onboarding program — for a professional photo lab. The work taught her something she has carried into every engagement since: you cannot build training that works if you don't understand how the work actually happens.

When she moved to Portland, she took a job as a press operator in a family manufacturing company — deliberately. Not because she needed the work, but because she needed to understand the workflows from the ground up, from inside the process, as someone whose job depended on getting it right.

"I investigate how work actually happens — not how people think it happens — then document what works and build systems that scale."

What she found on the floor was a gap between what training materials assumed operators knew and what operators actually needed to do their jobs safely and effectively. The maintenance documentation was incomplete. The troubleshooting guides didn't reflect real failure modes. The technology implementation guides were written by people who had never operated the equipment.

The Detective's Eye

Most training is designed by people who observe work from a distance. The result is documentation that reflects what work is supposed to look like — not what it actually looks like under pressure, with real constraints, in real environments. Becoming the operator first eliminated that gap entirely.

The Approach

Three documents. Built from the floor up.

1Investigate

Detailed job duty documentation

Rather than interviewing managers about what operators do, Kerri documented job duties as an operator — capturing the actual sequence of tasks, the real decision points, the things that go wrong and how experienced operators handle them. The result was documentation that operators recognized as true to their experience.

2Synthesize

Maintenance and troubleshooting resources

Built from real failure modes encountered during operations — not theoretical ones. Every troubleshooting guide reflected problems that actually happened, documented in the moment they occurred, with the solutions that actually worked. This is the difference between a resource operators refer to and one they ignore.

3Implement

Technology implementation guides

Written for the people who would actually use the technology — not the people who selected it. Each guide was built around what an operator needed to know to be productive, not around what the vendor thought was important to document. The result was adoption, not compliance.

This is where the methodology was born.

The Investigate → Synthesize → Implement framework didn't come from a textbook. It came from a press floor in Portland, where the gap between documented work and real work was visible every shift. Understanding that gap — and closing it through documentation built from the inside — is the foundation of every engagement since.

The principle
"Whether it's onboarding new employees, standardizing safety protocols, or optimizing workflows — I start by understanding the real work, then build practical solutions that people actually use."
The Outcome

A training journal that went worldwide

The onboarding work Kerri did at the press operator level led directly to her recruitment as a national trainer. The training journal she had developed as an operator — grounded in real workflows, real failure modes, and real decision points — became the foundation for the manufacturer's global customer onboarding template.

It was adopted worldwide to standardize equipment training across the manufacturer's customer base. Not because it was theoretical best practice — because it was accurate.

What this engagement delivered

  • Complete employee onboarding program built from operator-level experience — the first of its kind for the organization
  • Detailed job duty documentation reflecting actual workflows, not idealized ones
  • Maintenance and troubleshooting resources built from real failure modes encountered during operations
  • Technology implementation guides written for operators, not administrators
  • Training journal adopted as global customer onboarding template — used worldwide to standardize equipment training
  • Kerri recruited as national trainer on the strength of the documentation she produced
